Introduction

What Is a Septic Tank?

Understanding an important component of household sanitation.

A Basic Wastewater Treatment System

A septic tank is an underground, watertight container designed to receive and partially treat wastewater from homes and buildings that are not connected to a centralized sewer system.


It separates solid waste from liquid wastewater. Solids settle at the bottom, lighter materials float to the surface, and microorganisms help break down organic matter.

Why Are Septic Tanks Important?

  • Help manage household wastewater.
  • Reduce direct discharge of untreated sewage.
  • Support proper sanitation in communities.
  • Help protect groundwater and nearby water sources.
  • Promote public health and environmental protection.
The Science

How Does a Septic Tank Work?

Its treatment process involves physical separation and biological decomposition.

⚖️

Sedimentation

Heavy solid particles settle at the bottom and form a layer called sludge.

🫧

Scum Formation

Oil, grease, and lighter materials float on the surface and form a layer called scum.

🦠

Anaerobic Digestion

Microorganisms break down some organic materials in the absence of oxygen.

Treatment Process

Four Basic Stages

Follow the general movement of wastewater through a septic system.

1

Inlet

Wastewater enters the septic tank from household plumbing.

2

Separation

Solids settle while oils and grease rise to the surface.

3

Decomposition

Anaerobic microorganisms break down some organic waste.

4

Effluent

Partially treated liquid leaves for further treatment and appropriate disposal.

Common Issues

Problems in Septic Tank Systems

Improper design, operation, and maintenance can cause serious sanitation problems.

🚫

Clogging

Excessive solids, grease, and unsuitable materials can block pipes and system components.

💧

Leaks

Damaged tanks may allow wastewater to escape and contaminate soil or groundwater.

⚠️

Overflow

Blockages, excess wastewater, or poor maintenance can cause sewage backups.

Environmental Protection

Health and Environmental Impacts

Proper wastewater management helps protect people and natural resources.

🌱

Environmental Impacts

  • Groundwater contamination.
  • Pollution of rivers and other water bodies.
  • Excess nutrients entering the environment.
  • Damage to aquatic ecosystems.
🏥

Public Health Impacts

  • Exposure to disease-causing microorganisms.
  • Risk of waterborne diseases.
  • Contact with untreated sewage.
  • Unpleasant odors and unsanitary surroundings.
Proper Management

Septic Tank Maintenance

Regular inspection and proper use help maintain a safe and effective system.

🔍

Inspect Regularly

Have the tank and system inspected by qualified professionals according to applicable requirements.

🚛

Desludge Properly

Arrange desludging when needed and use authorized service providers.

♻️

Dispose Responsibly

Do not flush plastics, sanitary products, chemicals, grease, or other unsuitable materials.
