01 / THE BASICS

What exactly is a septic tank?

A septic tank is an underground, watertight container that receives wastewater from a building. It separates solids from liquids and allows microorganisms to break down some organic matter.

Why does it matter?

Not all communities have access to centralized sewer systems. A properly designed and maintained septic system can provide wastewater treatment where public sewer services are unavailable. Poorly managed systems can contaminate soil and water.

02 / HOW IT WORKS

The journey of wastewater.

A septic tank uses physical separation and biological processes to partially treat wastewater.

01

Wastewater enters

Wastewater flows from the building through the inlet pipe into the septic tank.

02

Solids separate

Heavy solids settle as sludge while oils and grease float and form a scum layer.

03

Biological digestion

Anaerobic microorganisms break down some organic material inside the tank.

04

Effluent exits

Partially treated liquid leaves through the outlet and enters a suitable soil absorption or further treatment system.

03 / SYSTEM STRUCTURE

Inside the septic tank.

The components work together to separate wastewater and retain solids inside the tank.

01 — Inlet pipe and baffle

Directs wastewater into the tank and reduces disturbance.

02 — Scum layer

Floating materials such as oils and grease collect at the top.

03 — Sludge layer

Heavier solid materials settle at the bottom.

04 — Outlet pipe and baffle

Allows liquid effluent to exit while helping retain solids and scum.

04 / RISKS AND CONSEQUENCES

When a system fails.

Improperly maintained septic systems can affect homes, communities, public health, and the environment.

01 / HEALTH

Pathogen exposure

Untreated wastewater may contain disease-causing microorganisms.

02 / WATER

Groundwater contamination

Leaking systems can allow pollutants to reach groundwater.

03 / ENVIRONMENT

Water pollution

Improper wastewater disposal can pollute rivers and other water bodies.

04 / SYSTEM

Backups and overflow

Clogged pipes and neglected tanks can cause wastewater to back up.

05 / RESPONSIBLE MAINTENANCE

Take care of the system.

Regular inspection and responsible household practices help prevent septic system problems.

✓Have the system inspected regularly.
✓Have sludge and scum removed when necessary.
✓Avoid flushing wipes and plastic materials.
✓Do not pour grease and harmful chemicals into drains.
✓Avoid excessive water use.
✓Keep heavy vehicles away from the tank.
✓Watch for foul odors and slow drains.
✓Never enter a septic tank because dangerous gases may be present.
06 / PHILIPPINE REGULATIONS

The laws behind sanitation.

Philippine laws provide standards and frameworks for sanitation, wastewater management, and environmental protection.

PRESIDENTIAL DECREE 856

Code on Sanitation

Establishes sanitation requirements intended to protect public health, including sewage disposal provisions.

REPUBLIC ACT 9275

Philippine Clean Water Act

Provides a framework for preventing and controlling water pollution.

REPUBLIC ACT 9003

Ecological Solid Waste Management Act

Promotes proper solid waste management and responsible waste disposal.

REPUBLIC ACT 1364

Sanitary Engineering Law

Regulates the practice of sanitary engineering in the Philippines.
