01 / THE BASICS

What exactly is a septic tank?

A septic tank is an underground, watertight container that receives wastewater from a building and allows solids to settle while microorganisms break down some organic matter.

WHY IT MATTERS

Not every building is connected to a centralized sewer system. A properly designed and maintained septic system can provide wastewater treatment where public sewer services are unavailable. Poorly managed systems, however, can contaminate soil and water.

02Separates solids from liquid wastewater

02 / THE PROCESS

From wastewater to treatment.

A septic tank uses physical separation and biological processes. Here is a simplified explanation of what happens inside.

01 / INLET

Wastewater enters

Wastewater flows from the building's plumbing into the tank through an inlet pipe.

02 / SETTLING

Solids separate

Heavier solids settle as sludge, while oils and grease float to form a scum layer.

03 / DIGESTION

Microorganisms work

Anaerobic microorganisms break down some organic material without oxygen.

04 / OUTLET

Effluent leaves

Partially treated liquid exits to a properly designed soil absorption or further treatment system.

03 / SYSTEM STRUCTURE

Inside the septic tank.

Each component has a purpose. Understanding the parts helps explain how the system separates and treats wastewater.

01 — Inlet pipe and baffle

Directs incoming wastewater and helps reduce disturbance.

02 — Scum layer

Floating materials such as oils and grease collect at the top.

03 — Sludge layer

Heavier solids settle at the bottom of the tank.

04 — Outlet pipe and baffle

Allows liquid effluent to leave while helping keep scum and solids inside.

04 / RISKS & CONSEQUENCES

When a system fails.

A damaged, overloaded, or poorly maintained septic system can create serious problems for households and communities.

01 / HEALTH

Pathogen exposure

Untreated wastewater may contain disease-causing microorganisms that can expose people to infection.

02 / WATER

Groundwater contamination

Leaking tanks or unsuitable disposal areas may allow pollutants to reach groundwater.

03 / ENVIRONMENT

Surface water pollution

Improperly treated wastewater can carry nutrients and contaminants into water bodies.

04 / SYSTEM

Backups and overflow

Clogged pipes, excessive water use, and neglected tanks can cause backups or overflow.

05 / RESPONSIBLE MAINTENANCE

A system is only as good as its care.

Regular inspection and responsible household practices help prevent failures and extend the useful life of a septic system.

✓Have the system inspected regularly by qualified personnel.
✓Remove sludge and scum when inspection indicates it is necessary.
✓Avoid flushing wipes, sanitary products, plastics, and other solid waste.
✓Do not pour grease, oils, or harmful chemicals down drains.
✓Prevent excessive water use that can overload the system.
✓Keep vehicles and heavy structures away from the tank and absorption area.
✓Watch for foul odors, slow drains, wet spots, and unusual backups.
✓Never enter a septic tank; dangerous gases and low oxygen can be fatal.
06 / PHILIPPINE REGULATIONS

The law behind sanitation.

Sanitation and wastewater management are supported by Philippine laws and regulations designed to protect public health and the environment.

PRESIDENTIAL DECREE 856

Code on Sanitation of the Philippines

Establishes sanitation requirements and standards intended to protect public health, including provisions related to sewage disposal.

REPUBLIC ACT 9275

Philippine Clean Water Act of 2004

Provides a framework for preventing and controlling water pollution and protecting the country's water resources.

REPUBLIC ACT 9003

Ecological Solid Waste Management Act

Promotes proper solid waste management and helps prevent improper disposal that can affect communities and the environment.

REPUBLIC ACT 1364

Sanitary Engineering Law

Regulates the practice of sanitary engineering in the Philippines and establishes its professional framework.
