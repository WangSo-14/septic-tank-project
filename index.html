01 — INTRODUCTION
01

Hidden underground.
Essential to everyday life.

Septic tanks are underground structures designed to receive and partially treat household wastewater. Through physical separation and biological processes, they help reduce the pollution load before wastewater receives further treatment and disposal.

Although often overlooked, proper septic tank management is essential for protecting human health, groundwater, and surrounding ecosystems.

02 — THE HIDDEN SCIENCE

What happens beneath the surface?

A septic tank uses several natural and physical processes to separate and partially treat wastewater.

01

Solid Separation

Heavier solids settle at the bottom as sludge, while oils and grease float to the surface as scum. This separation helps remove suspended materials from wastewater.

02

Biological Treatment

Anaerobic microorganisms break down some organic matter in the absence of free oxygen, helping reduce the organic content of wastewater.

03

Effluent Discharge

Partially treated liquid leaves the tank and requires appropriate further treatment or disposal, commonly through a suitable soil absorption system.

03 — RESPONSIBLE MANAGEMENT

Proper maintenance.
Long-term protection.

Responsible septic tank management helps prevent system failure, environmental contamination, and public health risks.

Regular Inspection

Have the system inspected by qualified personnel to identify damage, leaks, and potential problems.

Proper Desludging

Arrange appropriate sludge removal based on tank condition, usage, and professional assessment.

Responsible Waste Disposal

Avoid disposing of grease, chemicals, and non-biodegradable materials that may interfere with system operation.

Protecting what we cannot see is just as important as protecting what we can.

Proper sanitation begins with awareness. Every responsible action helps protect our water, communities, and environment.
